Nook cabinet

CNC plans and a build guide for a low cabinet that fills a wall nook, with sliding doors and a hidden drawer where the baseboard would be.

The wall is the back

No back panel. The decks run the full depth and touch the wall, so the cabinet uses every inch of the nook.

Doors need no hardware

Two plywood doors ride in milled grooves. A shallow recess in each door is the only handle.

A drawer that looks like a wall

The front runs to the floor, has no handle or notch, and the box uses about 91 percent of the depth. A hidden magnetic latch holds it shut.

Measure first, then cut

Plywood sold as 3/4 in is rarely 3/4 in. Every dado, groove and tab in these plans depends on the real thickness, so measure your sheets with calipers in three places, average them, and type the numbers here. The drawings, cut list and files below rebuild as you type. You can enter fractions such as 23/32.

Fit

    Starting values match typical North American hardwood plywood (23/32, 15/32 and 7/32 in) and the sizes you gave: 62.5 by 30.5 by 9.1 in, a 4.5 in baseboard, an assumed 5/8 in baseboard thickness, and a tight 3/16 in total clearance behind the drawer.

    Before you commit a sheet

    1. Measure the nook length at the top, middle and bottom. If it varies by more than 1/16 in, take the smallest number.
    2. Measure how far the baseboard sticks out from the wall, to 1/32 in. The drawer stops 3/16 in short of it, so a bad measurement either jams the drawer or wastes depth. If you are willing to remove the baseboard behind the cabinet, enter 0 and the drawer gains the full baseboard thickness.
    3. Check whether the baseboard also runs along the side walls inside the nook. If it does, plan a matching notch on the outside bottom of each side.
    4. Check the floor with a level. The cabinet stands on its two sides and the base deck, so shims go under those.
    5. Note any outlet or switch the cabinet will cover. Cutouts are easy to add to the decks before cutting.
    6. Cut the fit test described in the CNC section before you cut full sheets.

    How it goes together

    Five large panels do the structural work. The sides carry a dado, a rabbet and a slot each, so the decks drop into place and square the box without clamps doing the thinking. Inside, a center divider splits the cabinet into two bays, and each bay has four adjustable shelves.

    Doors

    The top panel has two deep grooves and the mid deck has two shallow ones. A door is taller than its opening, so you lift it into the deep groove, swing the bottom in, and let it drop into the shallow groove. Once it is down it cannot come out unless you lift it again.

    Drawer

    The drawer front is a plain slab that runs to the floor, with only a hairline gap under it. The bottom is a wide panel with tongues on both ends. The tongues ride in slots in the sides, which keeps the 5 ft wide drawer from tipping when it is open. The box rides on seven strips of felt let into the base deck, so it glides on felt and never on bare wood. Two screws through the mid deck stop the drawer at full travel.

    Locking and opening

    There is no handle, notch or keyhole. A commercial magnetic secret latch is screwed to the back of the drawer front, and a small strike block under the mid deck catches it. The magnetic key is both the release and the pull: hold it against the front over the latch, it lets go of the catch, and you pull the drawer open with the key. That also solves the problem of a drawer with nothing to hold. The latch is rated for wood 3/8 to 3/4 in thick, and the 23/32 in front is inside that range.

    Baseboard clearance

    Each side has a notch at the back bottom corner. The base deck stops short of the wall by the baseboard thickness plus clearance, and the drawer box stops 1/16 in short of that line. The box reaches about 91 percent of the 9.1 in depth, and the rest is the baseboard itself, which cannot be used while it is there.

    Divider and shelves

    The center divider is two 1/2 in panels glued face to face and seated in a dado in the mid deck and another in the top. It starts behind the rear door track, so the doors pass in front of it. Both sides of the divider and both side panels have two columns of 1/4 in holes, so each bay takes four shelves on shelf pins and you can move them every 1 1/4 in.

    Hidden compartment

    Inside the drawer, a thin divider sits in grooves just behind the drawer front. Pull the drawer out, lift the divider by its scoop, and a flat compartment appears. It is deep enough for documents, passports or a tablet.

    Key numbers

    Cut list

    Sizes are finished sizes in inches. Every part is nested with its long side along the 8 ft grain direction. The two doors are cut side by side from the same strip of the 1/2 in sheet, so the grain and figure run across both doors as one pattern. Cut the left door first, then the right, and keep them in that order.

    Materials and hardware

    Plywood
    One 4 by 8 ft sheet each of 3/4 in, 1/2 in and 1/4 in. The 1/4 in part is a single 60 in strip, so a 2 by 4 ft offcut is enough.
    Bit
    One 1/4 in compression spiral. A 1/8 in bit is optional for tight corners.
    Drawer latch
    One Lee Valley Magnetic Secret Latch (12K8001) and one magnetic key (12K8002). They are sold separately. Check the product page for the current mounting template, since the stem hole and screw positions come from it.
    Felt
    Self adhesive felt tape, 1 in wide and about 1/8 in thick, enough for seven strips as long as the base deck is deep.
    Strike block
    A scrap of 1/2 in plywood, about 1 in by 2 in, to carry the latch strike under the mid deck.
    Stop screws
    Two #8 by 1 in flat head wood screws.
    Anti-tip
    Two small steel L-brackets and wall screws or anchors suited to your wall.
    Shelf pins
    Thirty two 1/4 in shelf pins, four for each of the eight shelves, plus a few spares.
    Glue
    Wood glue with a long open time, such as Titebond II or III. Clamps or ratchet straps.
    Finish and wax
    Your finish of choice, plus paste wax or paraffin for the grooves, the drawer tongues and the top of the base deck.
    Shims
    A handful of tapered shims for leveling on the floor.

    Part drawings

    Each drawing is the view of the face that goes up on the machine. Horizontal and vertical positions in the tables start at the lower left corner of the part's bounding box. Dados, rabbets and slots that run off an edge should be cut with the toolpath starting and ending past the edge.

    On the CNC

    Everything needs one tool, a 1/4 in compression bit, and one sequence per sheet: small features first, outlines last. Compression bits leave clean edges on both faces of veneered plywood, which matters because you see both the show face and the cut edge on this cabinet.

    Start with a fit test

    Before cutting a full sheet, mill a 4 in strip of each plywood with a dado the same width as the one in the sides ( in), and a groove the width of the door grooves ( in). The sheet should slide in with light hand pressure and no rocking. If it is tight or loose by more than 0.005 in, adjust the joint clearance in the first section and the files will update.

    Cut order for each sheet

    1. Fix the sheet and set zero

      Screw down in the waste areas between parts, or use a vacuum table. Set X and Y zero at the lower left corner of the sheet and Z zero on the top of the plywood.

    2. Import the DXF and check the layers

      Each layer is a different operation. The table below lists what each layer means and which side of the line to cut.

    3. Cut pockets, shallow to deep

      Cut all pockets with the 1/4 in bit, inside the line. Apply dogbone relief to the mortises, pockets and tab roots marked for it, so that square tabs seat fully.

      Check one mortise with a scrap tab before moving on.
    4. Mark the pilot holes

      The pilot marks are smaller than the bit. Let the machine dimple the center with a plunge, or mark them by hand and drill 1/8 in later.

    5. Cut the outlines last, with tabs

      Profile outside the line. Use four to six tabs per long part, about 1/2 in wide and 1/8 in tall, and keep tabs out of the tab and notch areas. Add a final skim pass at the full depth for a clean edge.

    6. Free the parts and finish the edges

      Remove the tabs with a flush trim saw or chisel, then sand the edges lightly. The part names in the DXF are reference text only and are never machined, so pencil each name onto its part as you free it.

    What each layer means

    Starting feeds and speeds

    These are conservative starting points for a 1/4 in two flute compression bit on a mid size router CNC. Every machine differs, so test on scrap and adjust.

    Spindle speed
    16,000 to 18,000 RPM
    Feed rate
    100 to 150 in/min, lower on a light desktop machine
    Depth per pass
    1/4 in for outlines and pockets, two or three passes through 3/4 in plywood
    Plunge and ramp
    Ramp in rather than plunging. Plunge at 30 in/min if the software cannot ramp.
    Stepover
    40 to 50 percent of the bit diameter for pockets
    Dust collection and eye and ear protection are not optional on plywood. Cherry veneer dust is also a skin and lung irritant.

    Assembly

    Work on a flat surface and pre-finish the visible faces before gluing. Keep finish off the dados, tabs, grooves and mortises.

    1. Dry fit the carcass

      Fit the base deck and mid deck into the left side, add the right side, and set the top on the tabs. Everything should go together by hand.

      The front edges of the sides and decks are flush. The slots line up across the width.
    2. Sand and wax the moving surfaces

      Sand the top of the base deck, the door grooves and the drawer slots to 220 grit. Wax them with paraffin or paste wax. Do it now, because you cannot reach the grooves easily later.

    3. Glue up the center divider

      Glue the two divider panels together with the hole faces outward and clamp flat. Wipe off squeeze out right away, and test that the divider enters a dado dry.

    4. Glue the decks and divider into one side

      Lay the left side inside face up. Glue the rabbet and the dado, set both decks in, stand the divider in its dado, then glue and set the right side on top. Strap or clamp across the width.

      Measure both diagonals of the front opening. They should match within 1/32 in.
    5. Add the top

      Glue the six tabs and mortises and the top of the divider, set the top on, and weigh it down. Wipe any squeeze out of the door grooves right away.

    6. Build the drawer

      Glue the front to the tabs on the drawer bottom. Add the two ends, then the back between them. Do not glue the divider. Hold everything square until the glue sets.

      The drawer should sit flat on a bench with all four corners touching.
    7. Fit the latch

      Press the felt strips into the recesses in the base deck. On the back of the drawer front, drill the stem hole at the marked center following the latch template, and screw the latch on. Do not drill through the front. Test the key on the outside before going further.

      The key releases the latch from the outside, and the face of the front shows no mark.
    8. Slide the drawer in from the front

      Feed the tongues into the slots. It should glide with no sticking over the full length. Then drive the two stop screws down through the mid deck at the marked pilots so the tips show inside the drawer zone. Close the drawer, set the strike against the latch, and screw the strike block to the underside of the mid deck from above.

      The drawer stops at full travel and cannot be pulled out without removing a screw.
    9. Add the shelves

      Press the shelf pins into the holes at the heights you want and set a shelf on four pins. Shelves are loose, so they lift out for cleaning.

    10. Hang the doors

      Put the right door in the front groove and the left door in the rear groove. Lift each into the deep groove, swing the bottom in, and let it drop.

      Both doors close with a small overlap in the middle and slide without rubbing.

    Installing in the nook

    1. Test the fit before the final push

      Slide the cabinet toward the nook with the drawer and doors out. Check the side notches against the baseboard and look for outlets, trim or paint ridges that foul the back.

    2. Level and shim

      Shim under the two sides and the base deck until the top is level in both directions. The doors will drift if the cabinet is out of level.

    3. Anchor to the wall

      Fix two small L-brackets between the underside of the top panel and the wall, near the rear corners, with screws or anchors suited to your wall. Because the cabinet has no back, this is what keeps it from tipping when the drawer is loaded and open.

    4. Reinstall the drawer and doors, then run a final check

      Slide the drawer in fully and out to its stop. Slide both doors the full width. Adjust any rubbing by waxing, then by lightly planing the door edge, never by enlarging a groove.

    If something is off

    Dado is too tight
    Sand the tongue of the deck, not the side. Raise the joint clearance by 0.005 in and recut the sides if the whole sheet is thick.
    Dado is too loose
    Your plywood is thinner than the number you entered. Re-measure and regenerate the files.
    Door rubs at the top
    It is too tall for the opening. Plane or sand 1/32 in off the bottom edge, never the top, so it still engages the deep groove.
    Door will not lift in
    The top groove is too shallow or the door is too tall. The install lift check in the first section shows the margin.
    Drawer sticks
    Check that the sides are parallel. Wax the tongues and the base deck. If the drawer binds only near the back, sand the back corners of the bottom.
    Latch will not release
    The front is too thick at the latch. Shave about 1/16 in from the back of the front under the latch, or recess the latch, which the product allows. Check also that the key is held flat over the latch.
    Drawer front is not flush
    Adjust the joint clearance or shim behind the front's glue joint before the glue sets.
    Gap against the wall
    Nook walls are rarely straight. Scribe the back edge of a side with a compass, and sand to the line. Do not cut the decks, they define the track spacing.
