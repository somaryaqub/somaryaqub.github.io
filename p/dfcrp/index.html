A foothold, not a fix

Correctional Service Canada has opened a contract for Muslim reentry support in Edmonton and in the Greater Toronto and Hamilton area. It is small. It closes on 22 September. And it is the first federal instrument that would put this work on paper rather than on relationships.

We are not asking anyone to believe this contract funds the model. It does not. What it does is convert access that currently lives in individual friendships into an agreement with option years running to 2033, which is the base the larger national proposal needs in order to stand on something.

Thirty minutes. Five decisions. Nothing here is settled.

Sense of scale

Five hundred hours across a year is roughly a quarter of one position. For comparison, the single Toronto contract that has been running under this program has been about $281,000 over three years, with no wraparound services and no evaluation. This is a doorway, sized like a doorway.

It also carries real cost: an organizational security clearance, two million dollars of liability coverage, monthly reporting, and a named person who can be cleared. Any group that treats this as free money will lose money on it.

7–10 min

What the work is

Five task areas, written by CSC, that map closely onto what we already do.

  • Post-release correctional support. Liaise with the person, their case management team, and their faith community. Assess religious and spiritual needs, build care plans, coordinate transition rites, recruit and supervise volunteers, encourage compliance with release conditions, and engage people inside institutions before release.
  • Local needs assessment. Work with parole offices, institutional chaplains, and the existing justice sector, including John Howard Society, Elizabeth Fry Society, and the Salvation Army. Engage halfway houses and community correctional centres where Muslim clients live.
  • A documented strategy. Stand up an advisory group that includes local religious communities, police, CSC parole, clinical or social work practitioners, and academics, and convene it regularly. Build or adapt a reintegration support model.
  • Community safety and stigma. Educate the public, accompany people into faith communities, coach congregations to engage safely, and facilitate restorative practice where a community was itself affected.
  • Belonging and pro-social involvement. Connect people to activities they actually want to be part of, broker donations and material help, open employment pathways through congregations, and support coping and self-care.

What has to be handed in

  • A disengagement model within six months of award
  • Monthly quantitative indicator reports with invoices, within thirty days of month end
  • Quarterly narrative reports, within thirty days of quarter end
  • Two meetings a year with the CSC project authority
  • Everything in Excel, in English

CSC provides bookable desk space, a phone, and videoconferencing at the designated parole office. Nothing else is supplied.

10–13 min

What we are not pretending

Four things in this contract that a community partner should hear from us rather than discover later.

The disengagement language

The contract requires a disengagement model and asks us to promote a religious perspective described as non-extremist and rooted in mainstream religious thought. CSC defines disengagement as changing behaviour rather than ideology. That definition is defensible. The vocabulary is still the vocabulary of countering violent extremism, and some of our people will hear it that way.

Our position going in: we do not do intelligence work, we do not report on belief, and we do not accept referrals framed around ideology rather than release planning. If we cannot hold that line inside the contract, we should not sign it.

The reporting asks for individual-level data

The monthly template collects the last four digits of a federal offender number, date of birth, religion, a zero-to-five rating of the person's motivation to integrate into a faith community, and counts of our contacts with their parole officer. We would be scoring people for the government.

This is a consent and data governance problem before it is an administrative one. It needs an answer we can say out loud to a participant.

Clearance filters who can serve

The organization needs a Designated Organization Screening. Anyone touching protected information needs Reliability Status. Protected material cannot leave the site. Subcontracts that carry security requirements need written permission first. Volunteers and imams who cannot or will not be screened can still serve on the community side, but not everywhere.

It is a contract, not a grant

Work only happens when a task is authorized. There is no core funding, no separate overhead recovery, and a travel allowance of about five thousand dollars. Cash flow depends on invoices tied to completed tasks and accepted reports.

Decision 1

What we will and will not do under this contract

We need a shared line on the disengagement framing and on participant data, written down, before a bid goes in. Not a communications position. An operating rule that a coordinator can apply on a Tuesday.

  • Do we accept referrals that are framed around ideology?
  • What do we tell a participant about what gets reported, and when?
  • Who has authority to refuse a task authorization on these grounds?

Needed before submission. Owner to be named today.

Three ways to configure this

These are genuinely open. The third is the one most people have not considered.

A · Edmonton anchor

IslamicFamily bids Edmonton alone. Partners sit on the advisory group, host the community side, and take referrals. No Toronto bid from this table.

Needs one coordinator on payroll, four reference letters, clearance in motion.

Risk Toronto goes to someone with no interest in a shared framework, and the national argument stays theoretical.

B · Both streams, one bidder

IslamicFamily bids Edmonton and Toronto, and must be able to serve every location in both. Toronto delivery would run through a person employed by IslamicFamily.

Needs a Toronto hire or secondment onto our payroll before submission, in eighteen days.

Risk a thin Toronto presence scores badly on R1, and we would be bidding against the very organizations that have it.

C · Parallel bids, one framework

IslamicFamily bids Edmonton. An Ontario partner bids Toronto on its own paper. Both commit in advance to a shared model, shared training, common referral protocols, and a joint approach to national headquarters.

Needs a written framework agreement, and strict separation of pricing.

Risk we cannot coordinate bids without crossing a line, so the agreement has to be about delivery, never about price.

Decision 4

A, B, or C

This one shapes everything else, including who in this room is a partner and who is a separate bidder. If anyone here intends to bid Toronto, say so now and we will stop discussing anything that touches price.

Needed by 8 September to leave time to assemble.

22–24 min

The stack around it

Evaluation and wraparound are not in this contract. They still have to exist, so they get built alongside it on purpose rather than by accident.

Some of it is billable if we scope it that way

Work is released through task authorizations, and four things CSC already asks for are legitimately billable hours that also build our own capacity. We should agree the first year of task authorizations at award rather than take them as they come.

  • The disengagement model, which is a named deliverable due within six months
  • The local needs assessment, which is our baseline measurement whether or not we call it that
  • Convening the advisory group, which the contract requires us to establish and meet regularly
  • Building the volunteer recruitment, training and retention program

Stack the funding, do not braid it

Each layer sits in its own instrument with its own accountability, and they share one intake and one dataset. Nothing here depends on CSC widening the contract.

  • CSC contract pays only for the coordinator's authorized hours
  • Public Safety Canada community resilience funding for training, curriculum and convert supports, where we already have a track record
  • Provincial and municipal funding for the wraparound services people actually need: housing navigation, employment, counselling
  • Community philanthropy and waqf for the parts no government will fund, including family support and stigma work
  • University-held research funding through our academic partners, so evaluation costs sit outside the contract entirely

One intake, layered consent

The contract obliges us to report individual-level data monthly. Evaluation needs richer data than that, and it needs permission. One intake with three separate consents solves both: what CSC requires, what we use to improve the program, and what a participant chooses to contribute to research. Ethics approval runs through the university partner, not through us.

That structure means we can build a longitudinal picture that CSC cannot currently produce, since it does not disaggregate outcomes by religion, without asking CSC for data access we are unlikely to get.

Make the reporting work twice

The monthly template already counts contacts, accompaniments, restorative processes, donations brokered, and volunteer hours by organization. If we hold the source data in our own system and export the CSC subset, the same effort produces the evidence base for the larger national proposal. Volunteer hours in particular become an auditable community contribution figure sitting next to the federal spend.

Partners get agreements, not a directory

Every wraparound partner signs a short agreement covering how a referral arrives, how fast it is answered, and what comes back. Costs nothing, and it is the difference between a list and a network.

Then use the option years

Year one proves delivery. Year two produces the first evaluation output. Year three is when we go to national headquarters with data rather than argument and ask for scope and funding that match the need. The option years to 2033 are the runway for that, which is the whole reason a small contract is worth signing.
